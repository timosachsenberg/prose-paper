# ProSE publication: benchmark datasets

Status: draft, 2026-11-13. Every download link below was checked for the draft of 2026-11-10 (HTTP 200, size as listed). The decisions, from 2026-10-23 to 2026-11-13, are listed in section 5; the benchmark is run by scripts from one run table (section 4.5).

## 1. Main benchmark: 20 DDA runs, seven acquisition groups, full runs

This is the suite of issue [OpenMS/OpenMS#10364](https://github.com/OpenMS/OpenMS/issues/10364), with one change: every run is
searched **in full** instead of the 8,000 MS2 spectra sampled per file there. All numbers published in OpenMS/OpenMS#10364 and
the PRs that cite it are on the 8,000-spectrum subsets and have to be re-measured on the full runs.

**Input:** the vendor raw files, converted to mzML by us like every other dataset (section 4.0).
- **Frozen mzML files:** the mzML files in the table are the inputs frozen for OpenMS/OpenMS#10364 on 2026-09-30 (size and SHA256 from the reproduction package's `inputs.json`, [README](https://github.com/OpenMS/OpenMS/blob/a5705ec8e1cb19d40ebfa7bb16f6b5651aa1dc65/benchmark-reproduction/prose-andes/README.md)).
- **What they are used for:** they stay listed as the reference of the earlier numbers, not as inputs.
- **Spectrum counts:** from those frozen files (the package's `broad_benchmark/provenance/<id>.json`); they are counted again on the converted files.

**Totals:** 1,509,392 MS2 spectra; 1,497,401 of them with a recorded precursor charge of 2–5.

The three Astral files are byte-identical to PXD070049 (SHA-1 of its `checksum.txt`, compared 2026-11-11); the three HF-X files cannot be compared, as PXD028735 publishes no checksum. For the paper, cite the PRIDE accessions.

  - **Sample:** EDTA plasma of one healthy donor, prepared with the PreOmics iST-BCT kit from 2 µl plasma; 200 ng peptides per injection on a timsTOF HT.
  - **Digestion:** tryptic. PRIDE names the kit but not the enzyme; the iST kits digest with a trypsin/Lys-C mix, and Lys-C only cleaves after K, so the peptides stay tryptic.
  - **Authors' search:** Sage 0.14.3, trypsin without cleavage before P, 8–50 aa, fixed Carbamidomethyl (C), variable acetylation and Oxidation (M), 10 ppm precursor and fragment. The benchmark uses the shared tryptic search space instead (below).
- **Checksums:** the SHA256 of each frozen mzML is in `inputs.json` of the reproduction package (`prose-andes-reproduction.zip` at the README's commit).

### Database and search settings (as frozen in OpenMS/OpenMS#10364)

The ProSE parameter files that implement these settings are in [`params/`](params/) (section 4.1).

- **FASTA:** [ProteoBenchFASTA_MixedSpecies_HYE.fasta](https://archive.openms.de/openms/benchmarks/pride-benchmarks/lfq/QExactiveHF/ProteoBench_Module_2/ProteoBenchFASTA_MixedSpecies_HYE.fasta)
  (16,670,994 bytes, 31,889 targets, SHA256 `d9ac434d88492c10c8e9a587ee7dbc9480fa0995fa07a6ba35a7da8abf39aa25`).
  It is split by accession suffix (contaminants `Cont_` kept in all three), with one full-sequence reversed `DECOY_` per protein (the databases of the paper have other decoys, section 3.2):

  | Database | Used for | Targets |
  |---|---|---:|
  | `hy` (human + yeast) | Velos, Lumos LFQ, Lumos TMT | 27,488 |
  | `hye` (human + yeast + E. coli) | HF-X, Astral | 31,889 |
  | `human` | TMTpro, timsTOF | 20,767 |

- **Digestion and modifications:** Trypsin/P, 2 missed cleavages, peptide length 7–40, fixed Carbamidomethyl (C), at most one Oxidation (M); TMT6plex or TMTpro fixed on the labelled groups.
- **Tolerances:** precursor 20 ppm (TMTpro 50 ppm); fragment 20 ppm, or 0.5 Da for the ion-trap CID groups (Velos, Lumos TMT).
- **Counting:** target PSMs at q ≤ 0.01 after **ProSE's in-process Percolator** (the default since OpenMS/OpenMS#10578), as users run ProSE.
  - **Database for counts and timings:** the plain target-decoy database (`hy_td.fasta`, `hye_td.fasta`, `human_td.fasta`), as users search. The entrapment databases are searched separately and give only the FDP (sections 3.2 and 4.2).
  - Control, for the supplement: Percolator 3.09 as an external step (`-Y -U`, seeds 1/42/137), as in OpenMS/OpenMS#10364.
- **Entrapment, the only estimate of the true error rate here:**
  - **Velos:** natural entrapment, UPS1/yeast against all human proteins, r = 3.376.
  - **HF-X, Astral, TMTpro:** one shuffled, mass-matched entrapment protein per target, r = 1.
  - **timsTOF:** added later with the same `human` database (OpenMS/OpenMS#10639).
  - **Lumos:** only a whole-protein shuffle so far (OpenMS/OpenMS#10639).
  - For the paper all entrapment databases were rebuilt with DecoyDatabase, Lumos included; see section 3.2.
- **Comparison engines for the paper:** **MSFragger** on every dataset, **Sage** on every dataset except the electron-based runs (below).
  - Sage version: 0.14.7, the last stable release (decided 2026-11-10; also the version of OpenMS/OpenMS#10364). Version 0.15 exists only as prereleases (v0.15.0-beta.2 of 2026-03-31). The Linux binary of release v0.14.7 (`sage-v0.14.7-x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu.tar.gz`, 8,135,705 bytes, the size of the release asset on GitHub) prints `sage 0.14.6` for `--version`, because the release did not raise the version: at tag v0.14.7 the `Cargo.toml` of `sage-cli` and of `sage-core` still says `version = "0.14.6"`.
  - Sage is not run on the electron-based runs (PXD011695 except its HCD run, and PXD065289). No Sage release has z+1 ions: only the master branch has them (`z_radical`), and the `z` ions of the releases are y − NH3, 1.008 Da lighter (section 4.4).
  - MSFragger version: 4.4.1 (`MSFragger-4.4.1`, the version of its first runs on 2026-11-11: the smoke test and the first real files, section 4.5).
  - So far: Sage on the 8,000-spectrum suite, MSFragger only in OpenMS/OpenMS#10403.
  - How they are run: section 4.4.

### Caveat for the paper

ProSE's defaults were tuned on these 20 files (the PRs listed in OpenMS/OpenMS#10364).
A held-out set is therefore needed. The datasets in section 2 were not used for tuning the defaults and are that set.

**One held-out run led to a fix in ProSE** (decided 2026-11-13, section 5). The pilot of 2026-11-12 searched one run per parameter file, held-out files included. On a PXD019643 class I run it found a fault in ProSE's precursor recalibration, fixed by OpenMS/OpenMS#10886 (+22% PSMs on that run; section 5, task 6).
- **The rule:** the same for all engines. A problem the pilot found is fixed for every engine where the engine lets it be configured: Sage's fragment charges on the 0.5 Da ion-trap files, MSFragger's peptide mass bounds, the fragment tolerance of the immunopeptidomics files (sections 4.1 and 4.4). One exception was decided: MSFragger's search over its own settings (`calibrate_mass 2`, its default) could be switched off (`calibrate_mass 1`: +8.0% PSMs and +7.3% peptides on PXD042233; FDP still to be measured) or limited to the fragment tolerance (`calibrate_mass 4`), but it stays as MSFragger's default (decided 2026-11-12 and 2026-11-13). Its effect is documented and measured in the calibration supplement (section 4.4).
- **The paper states it:** PXD019643 caused a fix in ProSE, and the supplement shows PXD019643 with the builds before and after OpenMS/OpenMS#10886 (the first frozen commit `fbb25191d7` and `2287ee5532`) and with the build used for the paper (the commit frozen in task 11, section 5).

### 3.1 Tools

Since 2026-10-23, PR [OpenMS/OpenMS#10629](https://github.com/OpenMS/OpenMS/pull/10629) is merged into develop. It adds two tools:

- **`DecoyDatabase -entrapment_ratio N`:** writes N shuffled entrapment copies of each target protein (prefix `ENTRAP_`), each with its own decoys.
  - Each copy is shuffled peptide by peptide. The C-terminal residue and K, R and P stay in place, and residues are permuted, never replaced, so entrapment peptides are mass-matched.
  - Shuffles that equal a target peptide or exceed the identity threshold are rejected.
  - The output is deterministic for a given `-seed`.
- **`EntrapmentEvaluator`:** estimates the FDP at each q-value threshold. It uses the combined estimator `N_E / N · (1 + T/E)`, with T/E measured from the database (`-database`, I = L).

**How the design was validated (OpenMS/OpenMS#10629):** on HF-X, Astral and TMTpro it was compared with a foreign-proteome entrapment (reviewed *Arabidopsis*, UP000006548).
- The estimates were within 0.15–0.45 pp of the foreign reference.
- They were close to the OpenMS/OpenMS#10364 `make_entrapment.py` design.
- The ANDES whole-protein shuffle underestimated the FDP by 20–33%. ANDES has since switched to the same design (bigbio/andes#237).

**For the paper** all shuffled entrapment databases are built with `DecoyDatabase -entrapment_ratio 1` from develop, instead of the per-PR scripts.
- **Several realisations:** **3 realisations** (`-seed 1/2/3`) per database, because a single realisation scatters by 0.1–0.27 pp (SD) on 3 files × 8,000 spectra.
- **Reference:** the *Arabidopsis* foreign entrapment for HF-X and Astral (`hye`) and TMTpro (`human`), the groups on which OpenMS/OpenMS#10629 checked the shuffled design against it.
- **Archive:** all built databases are in this repository, under [`databases/`](databases/) (gzip). The authors' FASTAs are pinned by SHA256 in the build scripts.

## 4. Search parameters and measurement protocol

### 4.0 Input format

Every search, by every engine, reads mzML that we converted ourselves, all with the same converter and settings (PXD065289 needs a fix of the converter: "Trailer values of PXD065289" below; section 5, tasks 8 and 13). No deposited mzML is used as input, so no dataset depends on how others converted it.

| Source format | Datasets |
|---|---|
| Thermo `.raw` | section 1 except timsTOF; PXD011695, PXD065289, PXD042233, iPRG2015, PXD019643; set S1, the 250 pg runs; the independent UPS1 control (section 2.6); the HeLa HCD runs (section 2.8) |
| Bruker `.d` (`.d.zip`, `.d.tar.gz`) | timsTOF plasma (section 1), PXD058436, the timsTOF HYE runs (section 2.7) |

- **Records:** the converter, its version and settings are recorded here, together with the SHA256 of every converted mzML.
- **Spectrum counts:** the MS2 counts of all runs are counted on the converted files and replace the counts of section 1.
- **Converter:** OpenMS `FileConverter` from the frozen OpenMS build, for every format (the conversions so far: the first frozen commit `fbb25191d7`, whose converter code `2287ee5532` shares). The build needs `WITH_THERMO_RAW` (and a .NET 8 runtime) and `WITH_OPENTIMS`. Output is uncompressed, lossless mzML: no zlib, no numpress, no zstd (`FileConverter` has no option for zlib alone; it writes zlib only together with numpress, and `-zstd_compression` gives zstd). Sage, MSFragger and ProSE all read it; the files are larger than with zlib. The first converted file is checked for `MS:1000576` (no compression). Large files may use `-process_lowmemory`, which does not change the output.

**Thermo `.raw`: vendor peak picking.**
- **Command:** `FileConverter -in <run>.raw -out <run>.mzML -RawToMzML:reader inprocess`.
- **Reader:** the in-process reader (default on Linux and macOS): the `openms-thermo-bridge` 1.0.0 (pinned in OpenMS' vcpkg port, commit `75849ecf`) with Thermo RawFileReader 8.0.37.
- **Peak picking:** on, as by default; `-RawToMzML:no_peak_picking` is not set. "Vendor peak picking" then means, for each scan (bridge, `ThermoScanReader.cs`, `ReadPeaks`):
  1. **Centroid stream present:** a scan with a centroid stream (the label data of Orbitrap and Astral scans, computed by the instrument software) is written as these centroids. With them come the vendor's peak charges, if requested.
  2. **Profile without centroid stream:** a profile scan without a centroid stream (typically ion-trap scans acquired in profile) is centroided by RawFileReader (`Scan.ToCentroid`), Thermo's own centroiding in the library.
  3. **Acquired as centroid:** a scan acquired in centroid mode (typically ion-trap MS2) is written as stored.
  - In every case OpenMS itself does no peak picking. The mzML records `peak picking` as data processing, together with the RawFileReader version.
- **Precursor m/z:** the trailer value "Monoisotopic M/Z" if it is plausible, i.e. within the isolation window, or within −3/+2.5 Th for narrow windows. Otherwise the isolation target (`ThermoRawFileMetadata::selectedIon`). Both values are stored on the precursor. The charge comes from the trailer.
- **Trailer values of PXD065289** (found 2026-11-13): in the frozen builds the trailer values are paired by index with the file's trailer layout. Orbitrap Ascend scans (PXD065289) list only a subset of the fields, so their precursors lose the charge (0 of 41,365 MS2 spectra of one fraction have one) and the monoisotopic m/z; the precursor m/z is then the isolation target. The other Thermo instruments of the benchmark are not affected (every MS2 spectrum has a charge). PXD065289 is converted again with the fixed build (section 5, task 13). All inputs share one build only if the conversion of all inputs (task 8) uses a frozen build that contains the fix; otherwise PXD065289 is converted with another build than the other files, and this section has to say so. The fix changes only files whose scans do not list every trailer field: Q Exactive, Fusion Lumos and Exploris 480 files gave the same mzML as before; Velos, Elite and Astral files are not yet checked (one file each, spectra and precursors compared).

**Bruker `.d`: no vendor peak picking available.**
- **Why none:** Bruker's own centroiding of PASEF MS/MS spectra exists only in its closed TDF SDK, and OpenMS does not use it for spectra. OpenMS reads the TDF data with OpenTIMS and processes it itself.
- **Command:** `FileConverter -in <run>.d -out <run>.mzML -bruker:load_ms1 false` (a `.d.zip` is read directly; a `.d.tar.gz` is unpacked first).
- **Defaults otherwise:** the conversion uses the defaults (`bruker:ms2_centroid_algo off`), which means:
  - **DDA-PASEF MS2:** per precursor, the raw TOF events of all PASEF frames of that precursor are summed per TOF index, smoothed (±1 TOF index) and reduced to local maxima (±1 TOF index), and these are the centroids. This is the processing of timsrust, which Sage uses.
  - **MS1: not written** (`-bruker:load_ms1 false`, decided 2026-11-11). By default the MS1 frames are written as the detector records them (TOF events per mobility scan, not centroided). That is 81–96% of each mzML (12–18 GB per run, 7–8 times the `.d`), and Sage then takes 5–6 times as long as on the `.d`: the timed timsTOF searches would mostly measure mzML parsing. No search of the benchmark uses MS1 (ProSE's parameter files have no MS1 option; Sage uses MS1 only for quantification, which is off). Whether ProSE and MSFragger run as expected on the MS2-only mzML is checked on the first timsTOF run. The MS1 counts of the timsTOF runs in section 1 are the MS1 frames of the `analysis.tdf`.
  - **Calibration: without the Bruker SDK** (decided 2026-10-23), so that everyone gets the same mzML with open software only.
    - **m/z:** OpenMS' implementation of the TDF `MzCalibration` table, with per-frame temperature correction.
    - **Ion mobility (1/K0):** the rational model of the `TimsCalibration` table, or the linear model of `GlobalMetadata` if that table is missing.
    - **How the SDK is excluded:** OpenMS uses the SDK only when `OPENMS_BRUKER_SDK_PATH` names it (`FileConverter` sets no SDK path). The variable must therefore be unset during the conversion.
    - **Check:** the conversion log must not contain the line `TIMS calibration: Bruker SDK`.
  - **Precursor:** m/z is Bruker's `MonoisotopicMz` from the `Precursors` table, or `LargestPeakMz` if the acquisition software determined none. The charge also comes from `Precursors`, the ion mobility from `Precursors.ScanNumber`.
- **Checked 2026-11-11** with OpenMS develop `db4bd541`, on one run of each timsTOF dataset (`tims_plasma_30min`, timsTOF HT; `ultra2_A2`, timsTOF Ultra 2; `DN13_Tongue_classI_techRep1`, timsTOF Pro), against each run's `analysis.tdf`:
  - Every precursor with PASEF MS/MS gives one MS2 spectrum (115,047, 94,794 and 97,503), none empty; precursor m/z and charge are the TDF values; 1/K0 lies within the acquisition range; the calibration is the TDF tables', without the Bruker SDK.
  - Sage 0.14.7 on the mzML against Sage on the `.d` (read by timsrust), with the benchmark's configuration: of the PSMs at 1% FDR found in both, 99.7% (plasma) and 99.3% (`ultra2_A2`) have the same peptide, with the same scores. The mzML gives 6.6% and 3.0% more PSMs, mainly from precursors without monoisotopic m/z and charge, which timsrust drops and `FileConverter` keeps. 1/K0 differs by 0.01–0.02 (OpenMS applies the `TimsCalibration` model) and the RT by under a second.
  - Without MS1 (`ultra2_A2`): the same MS2 spectra, 2.3 GB instead of 12.3 GB, and identical Sage results.

The conversion settings (the versions of OpenMS, the bridge and RawFileReader) are recorded for every mzML, together with its SHA256.


### 4.1 ProSE parameter files

Every search uses one of the parameter files in [`params/`](params/). They are written by [`params/make_inis.py`](params/make_inis.py) from the defaults of the ProSE build, and the script checks each file by reading it back with that ProSE (invalid names or values abort).
- **Settings in the files:** the search space, tolerances, labels and charges, `decoys auto` and `fragment:min_matched_ions 4` (below). The immunopeptidomics files also set `peptide:enzyme_specificity none` (since 2026-11-13, below; it changes nothing in ProSE's standard mode) and a fragment tolerance of 20 ppm (since 2026-11-13, below). `low_memory` stays at its default, false, in every file (decided 2026-11-13, section 5; the immunopeptidomics files set `true` before, below).
- **Everything else:** the defaults of the ProSE version used for the paper. This includes in-process Percolator rescoring (`Search:rescore true`), the mass calibration with m/z recalibration (`Search:calibration:enabled true`, `recalibrate offset`), and the terminal rescoring features and stratum q-values (`Search:rescoring:terminal_features`, `stratum_q_values`).
- **Deviation from the ProSE defaults, `decoys auto`** (decided 2026-11-11): ProSE reads the `DECOY_` entries of the database, the same file Sage and MSFragger search, instead of generating its own decoys (`decoys generate`, the default). They are the decoys ProSE would generate (keep_termini, section 3.2). The reason: all engines search one file, even if ProSE's decoy generation changes later.
  - ProSE logs `decoys=auto: reusing existing decoys detected in the database (marker 'DECOY_', prefix)` and a note that `decoy_method keep_termini` has no effect.
  - Checked 2026-11-11: ProSE switches the terminal features and stratum q-values off when fewer than half as many decoys as targets (by share) start with M. None of the 36 databases (34 of the benchmark, 2 of the smoke test) meets that condition; their decoys start with M exactly as often as their targets. One search each against `smoke_td.fasta` and `human_td.fasta` logged no Met warning and recorded both options as `true`.
- **Deviation from the ProSE defaults, `fragment:min_matched_ions 4`** (decided 2026-11-11): a PSM needs at least 4 matched fragment ions, not 5 (the ProSE default). 4 is the default of Sage (`min_matched_peaks`) and MSFragger (`min_matched_fragments`), and the driver passes the value of the parameter file to both (section 4.4). The reason: the same value for all engines.
- **Current build:** the files were written on 2026-11-11 with the first frozen commit `fbb25191d7` (below), built from OpenMS's `dockerfiles/Dockerfile` (target `tools`); version string `3.7.0-pre-HEAD-2026-11-11`, revision `fbb2519`. **Charge range** (decided 2026-11-10): every engine searches a spectrum with a recorded precursor charge at that charge, also when the charge lies outside the range of the table. The range only gives the charges tried for spectra without a recorded charge (timsTOF precursors for which the acquisition software determined no charge, section 4.0).
- **Why:** Sage and MSFragger always search a spectrum at its recorded charge. ProSE skips spectra outside the range by default since OpenMS/OpenMS#10713 (on timsTOF, 5–6% of the precursors have charge 1). All parameter files therefore set `precursor:recorded_charge_outside_range` to `search`, the behaviour before that change.
- **Consequences:** this is the third setting, after `decoys auto` and `fragment:min_matched_ions 4` (above), in which ProSE does not run at its default; the immunopeptidomics files add a fourth, `peptide:enzyme_specificity none`, which changes nothing in standard mode (below). `low_memory` stays at its default in every file (below, and section 4.3). The published charge ranges of the immunopeptidomics sets (for example 2–3 for PXD019643 class I) do not restrict any engine.

**Sources of the settings:**
- **Tryptic search space:** frozen in OpenMS/OpenMS#10364 (`jobs.json`, arm `native_dedup:default`). All tryptic datasets share it and differ only in tolerances, labels and charges.
- **Immunopeptidomics:** the settings of the original publications (PRIDE data processing protocols), except the fragment tolerance (below).
  - **PXD058436 (MHCquant2):** 20 ppm precursor tolerance, lengths, masses, charges, and at most 3 or 5 modifications.
  - **PXD019643 (MHCquant 1.5.1):** 5 ppm precursor tolerance, lengths, masses and charges. The protocol does not state the modification limit, so the PXD058436 values are used.
  - **Fragment tolerance 20 ppm, for every engine** (decided 2026-11-13; published 0.02 Da for both). MSFragger replaces a tolerance in Da by one in ppm: from 0.02 Da it starts at 20 ppm and then chooses its own (7–25 ppm in the pilot, section 4.4). ProSE and Sage would search ±0.02 Da as it is, 34 ppm at m/z 588. With 20 ppm ProSE accepted 12% more PSMs on the PXD019643 class I run and 5% more on the PXD058436 class I run (fairness check of 2026-11-13). Sage, which also searches ±0.02 Da literally and has no calibration, would presumably lose in the same way; this is not measured, because Sage did not run on these files in the pilot (its unspecific index did not fit into 47 GB). The published "fragment bin tolerance 0.02 Da" of PXD019643 is moreover Comet's bin width: MHCquant 1.5.1 passes its `fragment_mass_tolerance` (default 0.02) to `-fragment_bin_tolerance` of the OpenMS 2.5 `CometAdapter`, which writes it unchanged as Comet's `fragment_bin_tol`. That is nominally ±0.01 Da as a half-width (OpenMS 2.6 and later state it as bin = 2 × `fragment_mass_tolerance`), so no engine would search the published value literally.
  - **Non-specific search, standard mode** (decided 2026-11-13, section 5): the main comparison runs ProSE's default, `low_memory false` (the standard mode), in the four immunopeptidomics files too; `low_memory true` is its own arm (below). The files set `peptide:enzyme_specificity none`, which `low_memory` needs: ProSE uses it only with `none` (`FragmentIndex.cpp`). The files written before 2026-11-13 set `low_memory true`, but had `enzyme unspecific cleavage` with the default specificity `full`, so `low_memory` had no effect (`peptide_mass_mode: false` in the end-of-search report) and the pilot searched in standard mode. In standard mode `none` changes nothing: on the PXD019643 class I run the final idXMLs with `none` and with `full` are identical apart from the recorded specificity, with the frozen build and with the build of both ProSE fixes (`622be5cd6b`; section 4.4 and below), at the same peak memory (14.4 GB; 2026-11-13). Memory: an unspecific human index does not fit into 12.5 GiB in standard mode; on the PXD019643 class I run it needed 14.2 GB, and on the class II runs it failed on the 47 GB pilot PC (`std::bad_alloc`). Whether it fits under the memory cap depends on N, which is not yet set (section 4.3). This is checked on the benchmark nodes before the main runs (task 12); if the standard mode does not fit there, the decision of section 5 is taken again.
    - **Measured on the PXD019643 class I run** (2026-11-13, frozen build, the parameter file of 2026-11-13, both modes): before rescoring, both give the same top two hits per spectrum. The scored candidate pools differ in 17 of the 4,346 spectra that reach the cap of 50 candidates (`scoring:max_candidates_per_spectrum`): one-for-one swaps of tied candidates of one protein at the same mass (site isomers or windows of the same composition), as the two modes break such ties differently. In 8 of them `hyperscore_zscore` changes, and Percolator amplifies this: at 1%, 4,069 PSMs and 1,465 peptides with `low_memory` against 4,070 and 1,480 in standard mode. Without the cap both modes give the same counts (4,193 and 1,547). The difference is smaller than Percolator's seed variation, measured with OpenMS's `PercolatorAdapter` (not inside ProSE) on the top two hits per spectrum of each mode's search without rescoring, counted with the driver's q-values: in standard mode over seeds 1–5, 3,933–4,104 PSMs and 1,452–1,513 peptides at 1%; between the two modes at seed 1, 0 PSMs and 1 peptide. `low_memory` peaks at 1.25 GB instead of 14.2 GB and takes about 53% more wall time (98 s against 64 s at 8 threads). So `low_memory` trades time for memory; its arm (below) is reported next to the main comparison.
    - **Tie order fixed in ProSE** (2026-11-13; commit `ba1f216fa4` on branch `fix/prose-lowmem-tie-order` of OpenMS, based on `3d697b06a5`; tested together with the runner-up fix of section 4.4 as `622be5cd6b`; pull request OpenMS/OpenMS#10896, opened 2026-11-13): the standard mode now orders tied candidates of one protein and mass by start, length and modification subset, the order `low_memory` already uses, so both modes keep the same candidates at the cap; the code path of `low_memory` itself is unchanged. One difference remains outside this fix: with `peptide:deduplicate` and two variable modifications that print alike (for example Acetyl (N-term) together with Acetyl (Protein N-term)), standard mode keeps one candidate and `low_memory` two; no parameter file of this benchmark sets such a pair or a modification that is both fixed and variable. On this run the pools then differ in 0 of the 4,346 spectra at the cap (17 before). With the runner-up fix alone (`445b5b0e64`, no tie fix) the two modes already gave 5,104 and 5,105 PSMs and 1,823 peptides each at 1%, with 137 final top hits differing; with both fixes both modes give 5,104 PSMs and 1,823 peptides, with identical hits and meta values (the final idXMLs differ only in the run date). So the 1% fewer peptides above are a result of the frozen build; the fix makes the results of the two modes identical, it does not recover peptides. The extra ordering takes about 0.7 s for the 134 million peptides of the class I standard-mode index (sorting them takes 6.5–8.5 s). On a tryptic search (`velos_5000_R2` against the pilot database `hy_td`, standard mode, before rescoring) it changes the kept candidate in 149 of 37,412 spectra and one top hit.   - **`low_memory` arm:** the manuscript compares standard and low-memory mode. ### 4.2 What is counted

- **Levels:** per file; for groups, summed over the files of the group.
  - **PSMs** at 1% PSM-level FDR (PSM q ≤ 0.01).
  - **Peptides** at 1% peptide-level FDR (peptide q ≤ 0.01): unique unmodified sequences (I = L), each with its best PSM. Not the unique sequences among the PSMs that pass the PSM-level threshold.
  - No protein level: protein inference differs too much between the engines.
  - **Decoys that equal a target peptide with I = L** (decided 2026-11-13): one rule in the driver for all engines. A decoy PSM whose unmodified sequence, with I = L, is a target peptide of the search space counts as a target PSM of that peptide's proteins (`il_twins` in the driver). The search space is the digestion of the parameter file: the enzyme's cleavage sites (Trypsin/P: after K and R; unspecific: every position) and the protein N-terminal methionine clipping; length, mass and missed cleavages are those of the decoy itself.
    - **Why:** the engines label such PSMs differently. MSFragger maps a peptide to every protein that matches with I = L, so it counts them as targets; ProSE keeps I and L apart in its protein mapping (`IL_equivalent false`) and Sage removes only exact duplicates, so both count them as decoys. That cost ProSE up to 1.1% of the PSMs and 1.7% of the peptides, Sage up to 1.4% and 0.8% (fairness check of 2026-11-13). The rule is MSFragger's and matches the peptide definition above (I = L).
    - The driver logs per result how many decoy PSMs it relabels (`commands.log`).
    - **Effect, recounted on the pilot results of 2026-11-12** (2026-11-13, no new searches; plain databases): ProSE 7–104 relabelled decoy PSMs per run, +0 to +1.1% PSMs and +0 to +1.6% peptides; Sage 23–123, +0.3 to +1.4% PSMs and +0 to +0.8% peptides; MSFragger unchanged, its results hold no such decoy (as expected from its protein mapping). All twins of the plain databases map to real target proteins.
    - **Entrapment databases:** about a quarter of the twins there equal only an entrapment peptide (on `hfx_A2` s1, 32 of ProSE's 135 twin peptides): a decoy, the reversal of a target peptide, can equal the shuffle of the same peptide, more often the shorter it is. Such PSMs now count as entrapment hits, as they always did for MSFragger, so ProSE's and Sage's FDP estimates rise: `hfx_A2` s1, PSM level, ProSE 1.19% → 1.33%, Sage 1.20% → 1.34% (MSFragger 1.18%); `pxd011695_EThcD` s1, peptide level, ProSE 0.50% → 0.76%.
    - **Sage and the Met-clipped peptides:** the search space comes from the parameter file, which clips the protein N-terminal methionine; Sage 0.14.7 does not (section 4.4). So for Sage the rule also turns into targets the decoys that equal a Met-clipped target peptide, which Sage did not search. On the 12 Sage pilot results these are 9 distinct sequences (7 exact, 2 only with I = L), 0–4 relabelled PSMs per result. At 1% Sage's counts are unchanged on 10 of the 12; on PXD042233 Sage gains 6 PSMs with the plain database and 9 PSMs and 10 peptides with entrapment s1 (at most about 0.04%). ProSE and MSFragger search these peptides and label them as targets anyway.
  - **Target PSMs that also map to a decoy protein** (since 2026-11-13): the same search space decides their label. ProSE's protein mapping goes beyond that space in three ways. OpenMS Trypsin/P also cleaves after X, in ProSE's digestion and in its mapping (the space, Sage and MSFragger cleave only after K and R). A peptide may start right after the initial M of a protein or one residue later, at position 2 or 3 (OpenMS's "M or MX" rule; the digestion clips only the M at position 1, as `peptide:clip_nterm_methionine` is on in every parameter file, so a start at position 3 lies outside the space unless residue 2 is K or R). And B, J, Z and X in a protein match any residue. A decoy candidate that matches a target protein in one of these ways is a `target+decoy` hit in ProSE and was counted as a target.
    - **Rule:** a target PSM that also maps to a decoy protein stays a target only if its unmodified sequence, with I = L, is a target peptide of the search space. It then counts with its target proteins of the space, so that `EntrapmentEvaluator` classifies it by them. Otherwise it counts as a decoy (`relabel` in the driver, which logs both numbers per result in `commands.log`). Target PSMs without a decoy protein stay as they are: ProSE's targets after an X lie outside the space (1–6 PSMs in 7 of the 20 pilot results, 17 in all, none accepted), and so do its decoys there. ProSE's in-process Percolator was trained with these PSMs as targets; the driver cannot change that.
    - **Effect, recounted on all pilot results** (2026-11-13, no new searches): on `pxd011695_EThcD` 3 of ProSE's 94 such PSMs lie outside the space with the plain database, and 3 of 160 with entrapment s1. ProSE's count there drops by 35 PSMs and 20 peptides (13,143 → 13,108 and 8,435 → 8,415; −0.27% and −0.24%), and with s1 by 16 PSMs and 8 peptides (the FDP estimates stay at 0.98% for the PSMs and 0.76% for the peptides). On `lumos_tmt_5058` 2 of 267 (plain database) and 1 of 392 (s1) lie outside, and on the pooled velos test 1 of 506, with no change of the counts; on every other ProSE result none. MSFragger has 9–1,070 such PSMs per result, all inside the space, so only their protein lists change, not the counts or the FDP. Sage lists no decoy protein for a target. Of the 106 search and level rows of the pilot, only these 4 of ProSE change.
  - **PSM-level q-values** (decided 2026-11-12): computed by the driver, the same way for all engines, from each engine's PSMs (the top hit of each spectrum, targets and decoys) and its final PSM score after rescoring: ProSE's Percolator score (`percolator_score`), Sage's `sage_discriminant_score`, Percolator's score for MSFragger.
    - Sorted by score, q = (D + 1) / T (at most 1), equal scores entering together, then the minimum over all lower scores.
    - The reason: ProSE's own PSM q-values are (D + π0) / T, with Percolator's π0 estimate (`Search:rescoring:use_pi0 true`, the ProSE default; π0 0.08–0.32 on three results), while Sage's `spectrum_q` and Percolator's (with `-Y`) are (D + 1) / T. On `astral250pg_A2` ProSE's own accept 926 PSMs and (D + 1) / T on the same score 916 (−1.1%); on `ultra2_A2` 40,583 and 40,576. For Sage and MSFragger the driver's values give exactly the engines' own counts (both files, checked 2026-11-12). Computed in the driver, ProSE stays at its default.
  - **Peptide-level q-values** (decided 2026-11-11): computed by the driver in the same way, from the same PSMs and scores.
    - A peptide is an unmodified sequence with I = L. Each peptide competes with its best PSM by that score, separately per label (target, decoy).
    - The q-values are computed as at the PSM level.
    - The reason: the engines' own peptide-level q-values are defined differently, so the same results would be counted differently. Sage's `peptide_q` is (1 + Σ PEP) / T on modified sequences, with PEPs from a kernel density estimate. Percolator's peptide table uses modified sequences, each charge separately. ProSE's `peptide q-value` (OpenMS's `FalseDiscoveryRate::applyBasicPeptideLevel`) uses unmodified sequences without I = L and ranks by Percolator PEP. The common definition changes every engine's count, in both directions. Searches of 2026-11-11 with prose-paper `5c5dd5f`, before the other changes of that day, counted both ways: on `astral250pg_A2` Sage's own `peptide_q` accepts 1,240 peptides and the common definition 1,303 (+5%), ProSE (647) and MSFragger (1,325) are unchanged; on `ultra2_A2` the common definition lowers MSFragger from 26,105 to 25,956 (−0.6%), Sage from 25,894 to 25,855 (−0.15%) and ProSE from 26,491 to 26,486 (−0.02%).
    - ProSE's own `peptide q-value` is not used, but nearly agrees on single files: the same peptides on `astral250pg_A2` (647 with the plain database, 631 with entrapment s1), 5 fewer of 26,491 on `ultra2_A2` (0.02%; ProSE keeps I and L apart in the competition).
  - **One counting tool for all engines:** `EntrapmentEvaluator`. It counts the top hit of each spectrum (PSM level) and distinct unmodified sequences with I = L (peptide level). With the plain target-decoy database its accepted PSMs and peptides are the identification counts.
    - The results of all three engines are first written as a minimal idXML by the driver (section 4.5): the target top hits with spectrum, unmodified sequence, proteins, the driver's PSM q-value (main score) and its peptide q-value (meta value `peptide q-value`, which `EntrapmentEvaluator` reads).
- **Database:** counts come from the search against the plain target-decoy database (section 3). The searches against the entrapment databases give the FDP only; their counts are not reported as identification numbers.
- **Rescoring:** ProSE's in-process Percolator (default).
  - Its random seed is fixed in ProSE (Percolator default 1) and cannot be set from outside, so the main numbers come from one run per file.
  - Control, for the supplement: Percolator 3.09 as an external step on the `-out_pin` of a second run with `-Search:rescore false -Search:report:top_hits 1`, with seeds 1, 42 and 137 (`-Y -U`). The mean over the seeds is reported, as in OpenMS/OpenMS#10364.
    - **One hit per spectrum** (since 2026-11-13; planned before: `report:top_hits 10`): MSFragger's `.pin` also has one (`output_report_topN 1`). With 10 hits per spectrum the external Percolator accepted 3.8% fewer PSMs and 4.6% fewer peptides of ProSE.
    - **The same rescoring for all three engines** (supplement, decided 2026-11-13, section 5): the same external Percolator 3.09 with the same options and seeds on the Percolator input of every engine, one hit per spectrum: ProSE's `-out_pin` (above), Sage's `--write-pin`, MSFragger's `.pin`. The engines' own rescoring cannot be switched off in the same way (Sage 0.14.7 always runs its LDA), so a comparison without rescoring is not made; this arm compares the searches under one rescoring.
- **Entrapment FDP:** `EntrapmentEvaluator -database <entrapment database>` on each result, combined estimator, at both levels (PSM and peptide).
  - **Every protein of a PSM** goes into the idXML, one accession each, and every accession must be a first word of the searched database (since 2026-11-13; otherwise the search is not counted). The `databases` stage refuses a database whose header first words (up to the first space or tab) are empty, repeated or hold a `;` or other white space (section 4.5): the engines cut the accession at different white space, and the driver splits the protein lists of Sage and MSFragger at `;`. None of the benchmark databases has such a header. MSFragger writes all proteins of a PSM into one `;`-separated field, which Percolator copies; until 2026-11-13 the driver passed that field as one accession, so `EntrapmentEvaluator` classified such a PSM by its first protein only and MSFragger's FDP came out up to about 10% (relative) too low on s1. MSFragger's entrapment results were counted again without a new search: on the eight s1 pilot searches its accepted counts stay the same and its FDP estimates change by −0.5% to +10% (relative; e.g. `pxd011695_EThcD` 0.78% → 0.86% of the PSMs, `iprg2015_1A` 1.28% → 1.33%). A PSM whose first protein was an entrapment protein but which also maps to a target now counts as a target, so the change can go either way.
  - Each shuffled design has three database realisations (`_s1`, `_s2`, `_s3`), each searched separately.
  - *Arabidopsis* reference: HF-X and Astral (`hye_arath_td.fasta`) and TMTpro (`human_arath_td.fasta`), one search per file. Not on timsTOF.
  - Reported is the mean ± SD of the FDP over the three realisations. Velos (natural) and the *Arabidopsis* reference have one database each.
- **Across runs** (decided 2026-11-13): an engine that did not complete a run must not change the comparison through the runs it lacks. Sage does not run on the electron-based runs (by design, section 4.4) and may fail on unspecific searches for memory (section 4.3).
  - **Coverage table:** every engine × run or sample × database of the run table, plus every further search folder (tagged arms, batch, thread scaling), with its status: `ok`, `failed …`, `failed_memory …` (killed at the memory cap or stopped by the engine's own out-of-memory error, section 4.3), `interrupted …` or `running …` (a search that did not finish), `not_run` (no folder), `not_run_by_design` (Sage on the electron-based runs), `unknown` (a folder without a status file, e.g. from a driver before 2026-11-13); `over_limit …` occurs only in the thread scaling. It also records whether each search was counted; `count` counts only a search with status `ok` (section 4.5). The driver writes it (`coverage.tsv`).
  - **Three-engine comparisons** only over the runs all three completed. **Pairwise comparisons** over all runs the two engines completed. A run is completed for an engine and database when its search was counted (`counted` yes in `coverage.tsv`; decided 2026-11-13, section 5).
  - **Summary statistic:** the geometric mean of the per-run ratios, next to the sums. Sums weight the large runs: on the pilot, ProSE / MSFragger was 1.014 as a ratio of sums but 0.99 as the geometric mean of the per-run ratios.
- **Immunopeptidomics:** unique peptides at 1% peptide-level FDR per sample, as the published references count them.
  - All technical replicates of a sample are searched together (`-in` with all replicates, `-out_merged`).
  - The driver computes the q-values of a sample across its replicates: the same computation as per file (above), on the pooled PSMs of all replicates (ProSE's `-out_merged` result, Sage's one search of all replicates, Percolator on MSFragger's pooled `.pin`). A union of the per-replicate peptide lists would not be FDR-controlled, as true peptides recur across replicates and false ones rarely do.
  - **Each engine in its best arrangement of the replicates** (decided 2026-11-13): MSFragger with one Percolator model on the joined `.pin` of the replicates (about 3% more peptides than one model per file as in FragPipe, close to the seed noise), ProSE with one Percolator model per file (`rescore_reuse:mode per_file`, its default), Sage with one search of all replicates (one LDA model). The arrangements differ, so the paper states them. Only immunopeptidomics samples are pooled, so every engine's arrangement is chosen on held-out data (MSFragger's on PXD019643), and the paper states this too.
    - ProSE's best arrangement depends on the hits per spectrum in its rescoring (section 4.4): with one hit `per_file` was its best. With two hits and the faulty runner-up features of the frozen build, `joint` (one model for all replicates) gave about 18% more peptides on the pooled PXD019643 class I sample (1,927 against 1,635; `per_file` 15% fewer). This is checked again with the fixed build (section 5, task 11), on held-out data as well (the velos test pool, tuned data, does not tell the two apart: under 0.5%). `joint` is chosen only if its FDP is checked (the class I mixed entrapment database of task 12). This rule of the check (on held-out data, `joint` only with a checked FDP) was decided on 2026-11-13 (section 5). If `joint` is chosen, it is a further deviation from ProSE's defaults (section 4.1), and the PEP ranking below and the decision in section 5 change with it.
  - **ProSE's pooled PSMs are ranked by Percolator's PEP** (`percolator_pep`; decided 2026-11-12), not by its score: with `per_file` each input in `-out_merged` keeps its own Percolator model, so the scores of the inputs are not on one scale, while the PEPs are. If `joint` is chosen (above), this ranking is decided again. Sage and MSFragger train one model on the pooled PSMs. In a test with two runs (`astral250pg_A2` and `ultra2_A2`), ranking by score accepted 26,562 peptides and 41,444 PSMs, ranking by PEP 26,606 and 41,529 (ProSE's own `peptide q-value` also gives 26,606). With one model both rankings give the same peptides (all 12 single-file results of the three engines, checked 2026-11-12).
  - No entrapment so far. How the true error rate is checked there is open: Jonas Scheid provides the design (section 5).

### 4.3 Speed and memory

Speed and memory are part of the paper, for ProSE and the comparison engines alike. There are three timing benchmarks. All of them search the plain target-decoy databases (section 3).

**Common protocol:**
- **Machines** (as in the manuscript):
  - **Engine comparison and batch arm:** a 64-core AMD EPYC 7763 node, reserved exclusively.
  - **Thread scaling:** a two-socket AMD EPYC 9654 node, threads pinned to whole cores, for all three engines.
  - Recorded for both: RAM, OS and storage.
- **Input:** local SSD. One discarded run first fills the page cache (in the thread scaling: once per file and engine).
- **What is measured:** with `/usr/bin/time -v`, per invocation, end to end, including the database index and the rescoring, without file conversion:
  - wall time
  - CPU time (user + system)
  - peak resident memory (maximum RSS)
- **Stages:** where an engine logs them, the times of index building, search and rescoring are reported separately. For MSFragger, the search and Percolator are separate timed invocations. A single file has two: search and Percolator. A pooled immunopeptidomics sample has three: search, joining the replicates' `.pin` files (about 2–4 s), and Percolator. In the batch arm there is the search plus one Percolator per file. The invocations run one after another. Per repetition, wall and CPU time are summed over them and peak memory is their maximum; the median over the three repetitions is taken of these totals. The memory cap applies to each invocation.
- **Repetitions:** each measurement three times; the median is reported.
- **Memory cap** (decided 2026-11-13): the same hard cap for every engine invocation, without swap. The driver runs each command in a cgroup with `MemoryMax=<N>G` and `MemorySwapMax=0` (`bench.py --mem-gb N`, through `systemd-run --user --scope`; needs a user session bus and cgroup v2 with the memory controller delegated to the user). Before each stage that runs tools under the cap (`convert`, `search`, `count`, `batch`, `scaling`), the driver reads the cap back from the cgroup of one call (`memory.max` N GB, `memory.swap.max` 0) and stops with a message if it is not in force, as `systemd-run` also accepts a limit it cannot enforce. A run killed at the cap, or stopped by its engine's own out-of-memory error, is recorded as failed for memory (`failed_memory`), not as slow, and enters the coverage table (section 4.2). Without `--mem-gb` (e.g. a test run) every failure is recorded as `failed` (since 2026-11-13): a kill by the kernel's out-of-memory killer cannot be told from other kills, so no engine's memory failure is labelled differently from another's.
  - **Out-of-memory errors of the engines**, read from the end of the step's log: MSFragger's full Java heap (`Terminating due to java.lang.OutOfMemoryError: Java heap space`, as the driver starts its JVM with `-XX:+ExitOnOutOfMemoryError`, which ends the run at once; MSFragger 4.4.1 has no recovery from that error) and MSFragger's own check before the search (`Insufficient memory!`); `std::bad_alloc` of ProSE and Percolator; Rust's `memory allocation of … bytes failed` (Sage). A `std::bad_alloc` comes from a single allocation the kernel refuses (its overcommit check against RAM and swap), not from the cap; memory that grows step by step is killed at the cap. The engines run as one process each (MSFragger briefly starts `ldconfig`), so a kill at the cap is a SIGKILL of the engine; with systemd's default `OOMPolicy=stop` a command of several processes would get SIGTERM and be recorded as failed.
  - **MSFragger's Java heap** comes from the same cap: `-Xmx` = N − 4 GB, so that the JVM's memory outside the heap stays under the cap. The driver refuses a heap below 1 GB, so MSFragger needs `--mem-gb 5` or more; MSFragger itself refuses small heaps (`Insufficient memory!`: on the smoke test at 2 threads a 2 GB heap failed and 3 and 4 GB worked). Without `--mem-gb` the heap is 80% of the memory the driver may use (the RAM, capped by the `memory.max` of its cgroups, e.g. a batch job's allocation). The driver logs the heap and where it comes from in `commands.log`. Before 2026-11-13, MSFragger was the only engine with a limit (`-Xmx`, driver default 200 GB), while ProSE and Sage could use all memory and swap.
  - **Memory-saving modes** are part of an engine's settings, not a way around the cap. The main comparison runs each engine with the settings of section 4.1. For ProSE that is its default standard mode in every file, the immunopeptidomics files included (`low_memory false`; decided 2026-11-13, section 5; before, these files set `low_memory true`, which took effect only once they set `peptide:enzyme_specificity none`, section 4.1). `low_memory` is the arm `low_memory`. On the PXD019643 class I run it lowered ProSE's peak memory from 14.2 to 1.25 GB and took about 53% more wall time; with the frozen build it also gave 1% fewer peptides at 1%, because the two modes broke ties at the 50-candidate cap differently, a difference smaller than Percolator's seed variation (section 4.1, also on the fix of that tie order, with which both modes give identical results). Whether ProSE's standard mode fits under the cap depends on N (on the 47 GB pilot PC it failed on the class II runs); it is checked on the benchmark nodes (task 12). Sage and MSFragger run at their defaults: Sage 0.14.7 has no setting that reduces the memory of its index (`--batch-size` only sets how many files are loaded at once). Any further memory-saving setting of any engine is reported as its own arm, with the setting named.
  - N is not yet set. It is recorded per machine with the RAM (task 7, section 5).
- **No network calls inside a measurement** (since 2026-11-13): Sage's telemetry is switched off (`--disable-telemetry-i-dont-want-to-improve-sage`; Sage 0.14.7 sends it before it exits, 1–2 s per run), and so is the update check of the OpenMS tools (`OPENMS_DISABLE_UPDATE_CHECK=ON`, at most 5 s once per 24 hours).
  - The flag removes only the network send. Sage 0.14.7 still collects system information when it ends (`Telemetry::new`, which reads `/proc` for every process and thread of the machine). On the shared test machine (about 120–140 processes and 515 threads) that took about 0.2–0.35 s per run, 0.2–0.9% of a Sage run there. It grows with the number of processes and threads on the host, one more reason to time on an otherwise idle node. It is in the wall and CPU times of `/usr/bin/time`, not in Sage's logged `finished in` time. No Sage option skips it.

**1. Engine comparison, per file:** every file of sections 1 and 2, one invocation per file, at 64 threads. Every engine builds its index anew for every file.
- **MSFragger's digest cache:** MSFragger writes the digested peptides next to the database (`<database>.<n>.pepindex`) and reads them in every later search with the same digestion settings. It would then skip the digestion that ProSE and Sage do in every run: in the pilot this made MSFragger's times 17–20% too low (immunopeptidomics about 20%). Since 2026-11-13 the driver removes these files before every MSFragger search, outside the timed command, in all three timing benchmarks.
- **MSFragger's uncalibrated spectra:** `--config closed` writes the uncalibrated spectra as `<run>_uncalibrated.mzBIN` (up to 1 GB) inside the timed search, and nothing reads them. The driver sets `write_uncalibrated_mzml 0` (since 2026-11-13). Checked on `velos_5000_R2` (plain database, 8 threads): the `.pin` is byte-identical with 0 and 1 (37,303 lines), and so are Percolator's tables; only the uncalibrated `.mzBIN` (408 MB on this run) is not written.

**2. Batch arm:** per group, all files of the group in one invocation at 64 threads, as users run a study. It shows how much of the per-file time is the index, which a batch builds once.
- **Groups:** the seven groups of section 1 and each held-out dataset. For immunopeptidomics, the per-sample search over the three technical replicates (section 4.2) already is a batch.
- **Reported:** time per batch and per file, next to the sum of the per-file times of benchmark 1.

**3. Thread scaling:** ProSE, Sage and MSFragger, each at 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128 and 192 threads.
- **Files:** one per group: `hfx_A2`, `astral_A2`, `velos_125_R1`, `lumos_lfq_5192`, `lumos_tmt_5058`, `eclipse_tmtpro_10855`, `tims_plasma_30min`, `DN13_Tongue_classI_techRep1`, `DN13_Tongue_classII_techRep1`.
- **Order:** from 192 threads down. The discarded run that fills the page cache is done once per file and engine, at 192 threads.
- **Size:** 9 files × 11 thread counts × 3 engines × 3 repetitions = 891 timed runs.
- **Time limit:** 24 hours of wall time per run. A run that hits it is stopped and recorded as over the limit, and the lower thread counts of that file and engine are recorded as not run, because they would take longer still. The unspecific searches at 1 and 2 threads are the likely cases.
- **Sage at one thread:** Sage 0.14.7 aborts when it sees a single core with its default batch size (it reads half as many files at a time as there are cores, 0 on one core). Its one-thread point runs with `--batch-size 1` on one core, like the other engines (since 2026-11-13; before, one search thread pinned to two cores). With one file per search the results are byte-identical (fairness check of 2026-11-13).
- **Reported:** wall time, speed-up over one thread, parallel efficiency, CPU time and peak memory per thread count. The 16-thread point serves as the workstation comparison.

### 4.4 Comparison engines

**Principle:** same search space, each engine's own algorithm.
- **Same as ProSE:** every engine searches exactly the same database files as ProSE (the plain target-decoy FASTA for counts and timings, the entrapment FASTAs for the FDP; section 3), with the settings of the ProSE parameter file of the dataset (section 4.1) translated to the engine. These settings are:
  - enzyme, missed cleavages, peptide length and mass. ProSE bounds the singly protonated mass [M+H]+ of each peptidoform, with its fixed and variable modifications (`peptide:min_mass`, `max_mass`), and Sage the neutral mass of each modified peptide, so the driver passes Sage ProSE's bounds less one proton (1.007276 Da; since 2026-11-12). MSFragger 4.4.1 applies `digest_mass_range` to the residue sum of the unmodified sequence: without water and without fixed or variable modifications. It gets ProSE's bounds less one proton and one water (19.017841 Da; corrected 2026-11-13), which matches ProSE and Sage only for peptides without modifications; MSFragger has no setting that bounds each modified form. The tryptic bounds (100 and 9,000 Da) cut no peptide of 7–40 residues. At the immunopeptidomics bounds the oxidised forms within k × 15.995 Da of a bound differ: MSFragger lacks those whose unmodified mass lies below the lower bound, and would search those whose modified mass lies above the upper bound. The upper bounds cut no peptide (the length limits bind first), so only the lower band matters. In the pilot the lower band held 4 accepted ProSE PSMs of one peptide on the PXD058436 class I run (GEVVPPAM with an oxidation, unmodified [M+H]+ 799.402), which MSFragger cannot search (about 0.04% of ProSE's accepted PSMs), and none on the PXD019643 class I run; no MSFragger PSM lies in the upper band. Class II is checked on the first real run.
    - **Before:** until the driver change of 2026-11-12 (commit `3592157`; the pilot of 2026-11-12 included), Sage got ProSE's [M+H]+ bounds as neutral-mass bounds and MSFragger as residue-sum bounds, so their windows sat 1.007 Da and 19.018 Da too high. Sage lacked the lightest 1 Da (0.01–0.05% of the immunopeptidomics search spaces), MSFragger the lightest 19 Da (its pilot class I searches: residue sum 800–2,500, i.e. [M+H]+ 819–2,519; its class II searches gave no results). From 2026-11-12 to 2026-11-13 MSFragger's window was 18.01 Da too high, so it lacked an 18 Da band at the lower bound (0.3–1.0% of the class I and PXD019643 class II search spaces; about 0.2–0.5% of ProSE's accepted PSMs and peptides lie there). The upper bounds cut no peptide with any of these offsets.
  - fixed and variable modifications, and their maximum
  - the configured precursor and fragment tolerances, as ± half-widths in all three engines (observed minus theoretical mass within ± the tolerance) (checked against the sources and on the mass errors of the decoy PSMs, 2026-11-12); all parameter files are symmetric. The calibrations of ProSE and MSFragger then change the fragment tolerance per search, MSFragger's also beyond the configured value (below).
  - charges and isotope errors
  - the minimum number of matched fragment ions, 4 (Sage `min_matched_peaks`, MSFragger `min_matched_fragments`; both have 4 as default, and the driver passes the value of the parameter file; section 4.1). The value is the same, the criterion not quite: Sage and MSFragger require it of the scored PSM, ProSE of the candidates of its fragment index, which holds no b1, b2, y1 and y2 ions. So ProSE PSMs can end with fewer matched ions: on `astral250pg_A2` 155 of 1,297 top hits, 21 of them accepted (checked 2026-11-12). ProSE also counts these matches over other fragment charges than the scored ones (below, "Candidate retrieval").
    - **Observed on a held-out file** (2026-11-13): on `astral250pg_A2`, where Sage and MSFragger accept more than ProSE, the rule explains the reversal. Weak spectra (most with fewer than 50 peaks) do not reach 4 matches among the index ions (`fragment:min_ion_index 2` leaves out b1, b2, y1 and y2), so ProSE gives a top hit for 1,297 of 6,024 spectra, Sage for 2,037 and MSFragger for 2,152. With `fragment:min_ion_index 0` ProSE would give 1,285 PSMs and 1,242 peptides on the plain database (default 916 and 779), against Sage's 1,002 and 1,015 and MSFragger's 1,326 and 1,276. On the entrapment database that arm gives only 558 PSMs, as ProSE's rescoring is unstable on this small file, so it is a sensitivity result, not the held-out count. **ProSE's default is not changed for the benchmark:** changing it because of this file would tune ProSE on held-out data (section 1, "Caveat"). It is a different mechanism from the peak limits on dense spectra (below, "Peaks per spectrum").
  - **fragment charges, where ProSE scores several** (decided 2026-11-13): ProSE scores fragment charges 1 to max(1, min(z − 1, `fragment:max_charge`)) when the MS2 spectra are not deisotoped (`scoring:fragment_charges auto`: `fragment:deisotope auto` deisotopes only at a fragment tolerance of at most 0.1 Da or 100 ppm), that is on the 0.5 Da ion-trap files (the ion-trap CID files and PXD011695, of which Sage searches the HCD run); otherwise only charge 1. Sage scores charges 1 to z − 1 without a cap by default. On these files the driver passes ProSE's `fragment:max_charge` (2) to Sage as `max_fragment_charge`, which gives Sage the same scored fragment charges, 1 to max(1, min(z − 1, 2)); elsewhere both stay at their defaults. Without the cap Sage lost 1.0–2.8% of the PSMs and 0.8–3.7% of the peptides on velos, Lumos TMT, the PXD042233 Lumos CID run and `pxd011695_HCD` (on `pxd011695_HCD` 2.8% and 2.5%, Sage's own 1% FDR, measured 2026-11-13). A further loss came from Sage's retention-time model: it is trained on the PSMs that reach 1% by Sage's first score alone, and on these files that were 0 to 169 PSMs (on PXD042233 none, rsq NaN; on `pxd011695_HCD` also rsq NaN); with the cap the model fits (rsq 0.77–0.85; `pxd011695_HCD` 0.82). MSFragger chooses its fragment charge itself (below).
    - **Candidate retrieval:** the match holds for the scored charges. ProSE's fragment index retrieves candidates with charges 1 to min(z, `fragment:max_charge`) on every file, while Sage uses its scored range for its preliminary match as well. In both engines one setting sets both ranges, so the retrieval cannot be aligned without changing the scored charges. On the 0.5 Da ion-trap files the retrieval differs only at z = 2, where ProSE also matches charge-2 fragments. On the high-resolution files ProSE retrieves with 1 to min(z, 2) from the spectrum before deisotoping and scores charge 1; uncapped Sage uses 1 to max(1, z − 1) for both steps. So ProSE's retrieval is broader at z = 2, the same at z = 3 and narrower at z ≥ 4. ProSE applies `fragment:min_matched_ions` in this retrieval step, so the "4 for all" of section 4.1 is counted over other charges than Sage's scored b and y matches. Measured on `velos_5000_R2` (plain database, calibration and rescoring off) with `fragment:max_charge` 1 against 2, which changes only the retrieval at z = 2: 97.4% of the z = 2 top hits were the same, the broader retrieval lost the candidate with the best HyperScore in more spectra than it found one (501 against 88), and the number of z = 2 PSMs at 1% did not change (7,736). It gives ProSE no advantage.
- **Translated, not copied:** the values of the parameter file are written in each engine's convention. Example: the isotope errors (below).
- **Engine defaults:** the scoring, peak processing and rescoring of each engine stay at its defaults. The engines are compared, not search spaces.
  - **Mass calibration:** each engine runs its own default calibration (decided 2026-11-12, section 5). The three do different things, and the supplement measures what each one changes (below).
    - **ProSE** measures the mass errors in a calibration pass over the 10% most intense spectra, corrects the m/z per tenth of the run (`recalibrate offset`) and searches with the fragment tolerance |offset| + 4σ of the remaining errors, between 5 ppm (0.01 Da) and the configured tolerance. A tolerance that covers less than 95% of the matched fragment peaks is not used, and the configured one is kept (`Search:calibration`, section 4.1). The precursor window stays as configured. Some parts of a run have calibration PSMs that are more than a quarter decoy hits, which estimates a majority of false matches (e.g. a contaminant matched to targets and decoys alike). Such a part takes the precursor offset of its neighbours instead of its own. ProSE then logs `[ProSE] Calibration: in N of M RT chunks of the precursor model most calibration PSMs are false matches …` (OpenMS/OpenMS#10886, in the frozen commit since 2026-11-12). In the pilot it happens only on the two class I runs: PXD019643, 5 of 8 chunks; PXD058436, all 10, so one offset for the run. The 16 tryptic pilot searches (8 runs, plain database and entrapment s1) give the same results as without it. ProSE calibrates every search anew: on `astral250pg_A2` 11.38 ppm instead of 20 ppm with the plain database, 11.48 ppm with entrapment s1. These values come from the first frozen commit; the fix flags no chunk on that run (OpenMS/OpenMS#10886).
    - **MSFragger** runs `calibrate_mass 2`: mass calibration, then a search over its own settings on the run: the fragment tolerance from a grid (5–50 ppm; from 0.02 Da, 7–30 ppm; from 0.5 Da, 150–500 ppm) and the peak processing (number of peaks, minimum intensity ratio, intensity transformation). The chosen tolerance can exceed the configured one, and a tolerance in Da is replaced by one in ppm. On `astral250pg_A2` it chose 10 ppm with the plain database and 7, 10 and 20 ppm with entrapment s1–s3. In the pilot of 2026-11-12: 10–30 ppm on the 20 ppm files (plain database 10–20, entrapment s1 10–30), 150–500 ppm on the 0.5 Da ion-trap files (500 ppm is ±1.0 Da at m/z 2,000), 7–25 ppm on the immunopeptidomics files (then 0.02 Da, which MSFragger turns into 20 ppm and optimises over 7–30 ppm; 20 ppm since 2026-11-13, section 4.1; to be measured again with these files). As it chooses per search, its counts (plain database) and its FDP (entrapment databases) can come from different settings.
      - The search over its settings also sets `remove_precursor_peak` and `max_fragment_charge`. On the ion-trap runs PXD042233, Lumos TMT and `pxd011695_EThcD` it chose `max_fragment_charge 1` and 150–250 ppm. On PXD042233 `calibrate_mass 1` (calibration without that search) gave 8.0% more PSMs and 7.3% more peptides, which would put MSFragger ahead of Sage there. The default stays (decided 2026-11-12 and again 2026-11-13); the settings MSFragger chose are reported per search (from its log, which the driver keeps), and PXD042233 and an ion-trap run of PXD011695 are in the calibration supplement (below).
    - **Sage** does not calibrate and searches with the configured tolerances; its LDA rescoring has the precursor mass error as a feature.
    - Corrected on 2026-11-12: this section said before that every engine calibrates "within the same configured tolerances", which does not hold for MSFragger.
  - **Rescoring features that differ** (documented 2026-11-13, not changed): Sage's LDA uses the ion mobility on timsTOF data (+0.2% there); MSFragger's `.pin` has a feature for the number of oxidations (+1.1% peptides on velos); ProSE gives Percolator the isotope error as one linear feature. Each is part of the engine's default.
  - **ProSE defaults without a counterpart in the other engines** (checked 2026-11-12; documented, not changed):
    - Percolator rescores the two best hits of each spectrum (`Search:rescoring:top_hits 2`), so the rescoring can replace a top hit by the runner-up. Sage's LDA and MSFragger's Percolator see one hit per spectrum (`report_psms 1`, `output_report_topN 1`).
      - **Fault in ProSE** (found 2026-11-13): `delta_score` (best minus second-best score of the spectrum's candidate pool) and `hyperscore_zscore` (z-score of the best score in that pool) are computed once per spectrum and written to both hits (`ProSEHits.cpp`). The runner-up therefore carries the best hit's values instead of its own (its score minus the best score, and its own z-score): every runner-up row does by construction, including all 9,298 runner-ups that Percolator reported on velos. `ln_num_candidates` is also written once per spectrum, which is correct: it describes the spectrum's candidate pool (like Sage's `scored_candidates`). This costs ProSE identifications: with `top_hits 1` the frozen build accepts 0.1–2.6% more PSMs and 0.6–3.7% more peptides on the tryptic and electron-based runs, and on PXD019643 class I 33% more PSMs and 36% more peptides (pooled 26% and 50%).
      - **Decided 2026-11-13:** the fault is fixed in ProSE (OpenMS pull request, section 5, task 11). Whether `top_hits` stays 2 or becomes 1 is then decided on the tuned files of section 1 only, not on the held-out files, and the fixed commit is frozen again.
      - **Measured 2026-11-13** (fix on branch `fix/prose-runnerup-features`, OpenMS/OpenMS#10895: a hit below the best one gets its own score minus the best and the z-score of its own score; `ln_num_candidates` stays per spectrum; frozen build otherwise; the four tuned files of section 1 that cover ion trap, Orbitrap, TMT and TMTpro: `velos_5000_R2`, `hfx_A2`, `lumos_tmt_5058`, `eclipse_tmtpro_10855`; driver counts, entrapment s1):

        | Arm | Mean PSMs | Mean peptides | Mean FDP PSM / peptide |
        |---|---:|---:|---:|
        | frozen build, `top_hits 2` | 26,071.8 | 15,899.2 | 1.170% / 1.143% |
        | fixed, `top_hits 2` | 26,484.8 | 16,176.5 | 1.089% / 0.967% |
        | fixed, `top_hits 1` | 26,393.5 | 16,141.8 | 1.060% / 1.047% |

        The fix gains 0.8–2.9% peptides on every file. With it, `top_hits 1` and `2` differ by 0.2% in peptides and 0.1 percentage points in FDP, the order changing between files: a tie. **`top_hits 2` stays** (the default, so ProSE runs with its defaults). With `top_hits 1` the fixed and the frozen build give identical counts, as the fix only changes the runner-up.

        **Observed on a held-out file** (not part of the `top_hits` decision, which used only the four tuned files above): on the PXD019643 class I run (immunopeptidomics, non-specific, the parameter file of 2026-11-13 with `low_memory`; driver counts, plain database) the runner-up fix alone raised ProSE from 4,069 PSMs and 1,465 peptides at 1% (frozen build `2287ee5532`, 8 threads) to 5,104 and 1,823 (`445b5b0e64`, 6 threads; +25% and +24%). The two builds differ in ProSE's code only by the runner-up fix (the commit between them, `3d697b06a5`, changes only UniPEFF), and the thread count does not change the result (the frozen build's standard mode gave 4,070 PSMs and 1,480 peptides at 8 and at 6 threads). No entrapment FDP exists for this file yet.
    - No minimum number of peaks per spectrum. Sage and MSFragger skip spectra with fewer than 15 peaks (`min_peaks`, `minimum_peaks`). On `astral250pg_A2` such spectra gave 12 of ProSE's 926 accepted PSMs (21 when only the peaks in Sage's fragment range of 150–2,000 m/z are counted; ProSE's own q-values, before the PSM level of 2026-11-12).
    - Self-trained fragment-ion priors (`Search:annotate:self_trained_ion_priors true`): fragment-ion likelihoods learned from the confident PSMs of the run itself, cross-validated over two halves of the spectra, as Percolator features. Like Sage's retention-time model (below), they are trained on the search itself, not an external predictor, so they stay on.
  - **Retention time in Sage's rescoring:** Sage's `predict_rt` (default true) fits a retention-time model on the confident PSMs (1% FDR) of the search itself and uses the deviation as a rescoring feature: per run in the per-file searches; in a search of several files (the replicates of an immunopeptidomics sample, section 4.2) one model on the retention times of all files after aligning them (Sage 0.14.7, `sage-cli/src/main.rs`, `ml/retention_model.rs`). It is not an external predictor like MSBooster or PeptDeep (below), so it stays on.
- **Counting:** the same for all engines (section 4.2), including the entrapment FDP with `EntrapmentEvaluator`.

**Differences in the search space that remain** (checked against the sources on 2026-11-10):
- **Electron-based runs:** ProSE scores spectra recorded as electron-activated with b, y, c and z+1 ions (`Search:ions:by_activation`). MSFragger gets `fragment_ion_series = b,y,c,z`, as in FragPipe's hybrid workflows. Sage is not run there (section 1).
- **N-terminal methionine:** ProSE and MSFragger also search protein N-terminal peptides without the initial methionine (`peptide:clip_nterm_methionine`, `clip_nTerm_M`). Sage 0.14.7 has no such option, so it lacks these peptides. The count's search space holds them for every engine (section 4.2).
- **ProSE's digestion and protein mapping:** OpenMS Trypsin/P also cleaves after X, in ProSE's digestion and in its protein mapping; Sage and MSFragger cleave only after K and R. ProSE's mapping also accepts a peptide that starts right after the initial M of a protein or one residue later, at position 2 or 3 (OpenMS's "M or MX" rule; the digestion clips only the M at position 1), and it matches B, J, Z and X in a protein as wildcards. The count takes the target and decoy labels of every engine from one search space, which cleaves only after K and R (section 4.2), so what remains is that ProSE also searches the peptides after an X: 1–6 target PSMs in 7 of the 20 pilot results (17 in all), none accepted, with decoys of the same kind. The protein lists of ProSE's other target PSMs still come from its own mapping: a peptide of an entrapment protein that the M/MX rule or a wildcard also maps to an original target protein would count as a target hit (none in the s1 pilot results).
- **Isotope errors:** −2…1 in all parameter files. The tryptic files set them; the immunopeptidomics files keep the ProSE default, which is −2…1 in the frozen build (−1…1 before 2026-11-02).
  - **Conventions:** ProSE adds the isotope error to the observed mass (−1 finds a peptide whose first 13C peak was selected as the precursor). Sage and MSFragger count observed minus theoretical mass, so the driver negates the range: ProSE −2…1 is `isotope_errors [-1, 2]` for Sage and `isotope_error -1/0/1/2` for MSFragger. All three search the same precursors: up to two 13C spacings above the monoisotopic mass and one below.
  - **Fixed on 2026-11-11:** before, the driver passed −2…1 unchanged, so Sage and MSFragger searched observed − theoretical −2…+1 while ProSE searched −1…+2 (section 5).
- **Charge range:** every engine searches every spectrum at its recorded charge (section 4.1).
- **Sage version string:** the binary of release v0.14.7 reports `sage 0.14.6` (the release did not raise its version, section 1).
- **MSFragger parameter file:** the driver writes the settings over the defaults of `MSFragger --config closed`, and removes its default variable modifications (N-terminal acetylation among them).
- **TMT reporter ions:** MSFragger searches the TMT spectra with the reporter-ion region (`clear_mz_range 0 0`, the closed-search default); FragPipe's TMT workflows remove 125.5–134.5 m/z. Sage's fragment range starts at 150 m/z. To be measured on one TMTpro run before the main runs (section 5, task 12); the estimate is at most +0.2–0.5% for MSFragger.
  - **Measured 2026-11-13** on `eclipse_tmtpro_10855` (frozen build, plain database): with 125.5–134.5 m/z removed MSFragger accepts 11,816 PSMs and 8,935 peptides, without 11,848 and 8,941. Removing the region does not help MSFragger here, so the closed-search default stays.
- **Peaks per spectrum** (not the search space, but the peaks each engine uses; each engine's default, not translated, decided 2026-11-13, section 5; measured 2026-11-13 on `astral_A2`):
  - **Sage** uses the 150 most intense deisotoped peaks in 150–2,000 m/z (`max_peaks` 150, its default) for its preliminary match, its scoring and its LDA features.
  - **MSFragger** uses its `use_topN_peaks` most intense peaks. `calibrate_mass 2` chooses the value from a grid of 100–300 and overrides a configured one. On `astral_A2` it chose 300, the top of the grid (in its calibration pass 34,077 PSMs at 300 peaks, 31,716 at 200).
  - **ProSE** looks up candidates with all raw peaks (`fragment:query_spectrum auto`: raw at 100 ppm or less). It scores at most 20 peaks per 100 Th (`peaks:window_top`), capped by `peaks:keep_n` (auto: about 400 at high resolution; on `astral_A2` about 190 peaks per spectrum, estimated, so the cap does not bind). It computes its ion-prior and local fragment evidence features on all deisotoped peaks, before these filters (`annotate:ion_prior_peaks all`, `annotate:local_fragment_evidence`, the Percolator features `chance_match_surprise` and `mass_competition_evidence`). On `astral_A2`, switching the ion priors off costs ProSE 3,312 PSMs, and switching the local fragment evidence off costs 914 PSMs (3,311 in an arm limited to Sage's peak budget of 150, where it is the only feature that still sees all peaks).
  - **Cost on `astral_A2`** (driver counts, plain database): Sage with `max_peaks` 300 gives +7.0% PSMs / +5.1% peptides, 400 +7.9% to +9.5% / +6.0% to +6.7%, 500 +10.5% / +7.1%. The range at 400 comes from three runs with the same settings (37,051, 37,569 and 37,616 PSMs): their top hits and features are identical, only the discriminant of Sage's LDA differs. Repeats at 150, 300 and 500 peaks on `astral_A2` and at 400 on `hfx_A2` gave identical counts. `max_peaks` 2,000 gives fewer than 400 (35,976 PSMs, 21,137 peptides). MSFragger with 500 peaks against 300, both with `calibrate_mass 1` at its chosen 7 ppm: +5.6% / +3.9%, and +5.0% / +3.1% at a matched combined FDP of 1.0% (entrapment s1).
  - **Depends on spectrum density:** the median number of raw peaks per MS2 spectrum is 978 on `astral_A2`, 173 on `hfx_A2` and 25 on `astral250pg_A2`. At `max_peaks` 500 Sage gains 1.4% / 0.9% on `hfx_A2` and loses 0.6% / 1.0% on `tims_plasma_30min` (one run). `astral_A2` is one of the files ProSE's defaults were tuned on (section 1, "Caveat"), its peak settings included.
  - **ProSE's peak cap given to Sage on the nine pilot files where Sage runs** (measured 2026-11-13; `--extra max_peaks=<cap>` against Sage's main arm with the same driver; ProSE's `peaks:keep_n` 0 resolves to 400 at 20 ppm and to 80 at 0.5 Da; driver counts, plain database; combined FDP with entrapment s1): only `astral_A2` gains clearly, +7.9% to +9.5% PSMs and +6.0% to +6.7% peptides (the three runs at 400 above). On the other eight files the counts change by −1.1% to +3.5% (PSMs; +3.5% is 35 PSMs on `astral250pg_A2`) and by −2.1% to +2.4% (peptides), in both directions; repeated runs gave the same counts within 2 PSMs. On the three 0.5 Da files the cap of 80 lies below Sage's default of 150, so there the translation would take peaks away from Sage. The combined FDP changes by at most 0.51 percentage points, and each of the 18 changes (nine files, PSM and peptide level) lies within the Poisson noise of the entrapment counts (|z| ≤ 1.5); on `astral_A2` it is 1.23% / 1.27% against 1.38% / 1.33%. ProSE itself scores fewer peaks than its cap on most high-resolution spectra (about 190 on `astral_A2` after its window filter), so `max_peaks` 400 gives Sage more scored peaks than ProSE there.
- **Further engine behaviour on `astral_A2`** (found 2026-11-13; engine behaviour, not driver faults):
  - **Fragment tolerance and peaks interact:** Sage searches the uncalibrated 20 ppm (above, "Mass calibration"). With 400 peaks and ±6.26 ppm Sage gives 40,937 PSMs and 23,949 peptides, 1.2% / 1.0% below MSFragger.
  - **FDP at q ≤ 0.01:** ProSE runs at a higher estimated combined entrapment FDP than MSFragger: PSMs 1.41% against 1.09% (difference 0.32 points, interval 0.12 to 0.49), peptides 1.45% against 1.24% (within noise). On entrapment s1 the ProSE–MSFragger gap is 12.6% of the PSMs at a matched combined FDP of 1.0% (42,195 against 37,469) instead of 17.8% at q ≤ 0.01 (44,650 against 37,904); for peptides it is 10.3% (24,115 against 21,854) instead of 12.4% (25,372 against 22,574), a change within noise. On the plain database the gap at q ≤ 0.01 is 17.7% and 12.6% (section 4.5).
  - **Isotope errors:** ProSE's Percolator and Sage's LDA take the isotope error as one signed linear feature, MSFragger's `.pin` as one-hot features. ProSE's and Sage's accepted PSMs at a non-zero isotope error have a combined FDP of 2.8% and 2.3%, MSFragger's 1.3%; at isotope error 0 all three lie at 1.05–1.26%. The searched range is the same in all three (above).

**Without predictions in the main comparison:** MSBooster adds features from predicted spectra and retention times. ProSE does not use predictions by default (`Search:peptdeep:enable false`), so the main comparison runs both without.

### 4.5 Scripts: every raw number comes from a script

The benchmark is driven by scripts, so that it can be repeated with one command per stage and nobody selects files or types settings by hand.

- **Run table:** [`../benchmarks/runs.tsv`](../benchmarks/runs.tsv) has one row per run of sections 1 and 2 (92 runs): ID, set, group, download link, parameter file, databases, and the flags for the scaling files and the immunopeptidomics samples. The tables of this document and the run table must agree; the run table is what the scripts read.
- **Search settings:** the ProSE parameter files in [`params/`](params/) are the single source. The driver translates the Sage and MSFragger configurations from the values in these files, including the ProSE defaults that [`params/make_inis.py`](params/make_inis.py) does not set (for example the isotope errors −2…1 of the immunopeptidomics files and the clipping of the protein N-terminal methionine). So the three engines cannot drift apart.
  - **Translated in each engine's convention,** not copied: for example the isotope errors (section 4.4).
  - **Not translated**, each engine keeps its default: fragment m/z range, fragment charges (except on the 0.5 Da ion-trap files, below), deisotoping (Sage's costs it no identifications on the 0.5 Da ion-trap data, checked in the pilot; section 5, task 6), mass calibration (its effect is the supplement of section 4.4). The minimum number of matched fragment ions is translated since 2026-11-11 (4 for all, section 4.1), the peptide mass bounds since 2026-11-12 (in each engine's convention, MSFragger's corrected 2026-11-13; section 4.4), and ProSE's maximum fragment charge to Sage where ProSE scores several fragment charges since 2026-11-13 (section 4.4).
- **Driver:** [`../benchmarks/bench.py`](../benchmarks/bench.py) (Linux, Python 3 standard library only), with one subcommand per stage. [`../benchmarks/README.md`](../benchmarks/README.md) has the commands in their order.

  | Stage | What it does |
  |---|---|
  | `versions` | records the version output of every tool |
  | `databases` | unpacks the databases of the run table from [`databases/`](databases/), checks their SHA256, and checks that every header has an accession all engines read alike (first word up to the first space or tab: not empty, not repeated, no `;` or other white space) |
  | `fetch` | downloads the vendor files of the run table and checks every file, also one already there, against the SHA256 in `vendor.sha256` next to the run table |
  | `convert` | converts them to mzML with `FileConverter` (section 4.0), keeps its output as the record of the conversion, and records the SHA256 of every mzML |
  | `search` | runs one engine on every run against the plain target-decoy, the entrapment or the reference databases, each search in an emptied folder with its `status`; with `--timed`, one discarded run and three repetitions under `/usr/bin/time -v` |
  | `count` | takes the searches with status `ok`, computes the PSM- and peptide-level q-values the same way for every engine, writes each result as idXML and runs `EntrapmentEvaluator` on it: counts with the plain target-decoy database, FDP with the entrapment databases (section 4.2) |
  | `collect` | joins all counts into one table (`counts.tsv`) and writes the coverage table (`coverage.tsv`, section 4.2) |
  | `batch` | the batch arm of section 4.3 (groups of at least two files) |
  | `scaling` | the thread-scaling grid of section 4.3, pinned, with the time limit |

- **Vendor checksums:** [`../benchmarks/vendor.sha256`](../benchmarks/vendor.sha256) holds the SHA256 of all 92 vendor files of the 92 runs, computed on 2026-11-11 from copies that were checked against the sources:
  - 80 files match the SHA-1 that PRIDE publishes: in the project's `checksum.txt` (PXD053748, PXD058436, PXD065289, PXD070049; 37 files) or through the PRIDE Archive API (PXD007683, PXD010981, PXD011695, PXD019643, PXD042233; 40 files), and the 3 ProteoBench Astral copies against PXD070049.
  - The 12 raw files without a published checksum (PXD001819: 6, PXD054559: 3, ProteoBench HF-X copies: 3) were downloaded twice, with the same SHA256.
  - A right size alone does not prove a good download: one file of PXD058436 had the right size, but 196 wrong bytes and a failing gzip test. `urllib` only notices a download that ends short.
- **Records:** every timed invocation appends one line to `timings.tsv` (engine, benchmark, run, database, stage, threads, repetition, wall time, CPU time, peak memory, exit status); repetition 0 is the discarded run. Every invocation has its own time file (since 2026-11-13), so drivers that share a work directory do not disturb each other's timings, and the header of `timings.tsv` is written under a file lock, so it stays the first line; a run whose time `/usr/bin/time` did not deliver is recorded with `NA` and exit `no_timing` (and `# NO TIMING` in `commands.log`), never as 0 s. Every stage writes its exact command lines to `commands.log`, and `--dry-run` prints them without running anything.
- **Status of every search** (since 2026-11-13): a search first empties its folder, then writes `inputs` (the run ids it searches) and `status`. The status reads `running …` (with the process ids of the driver and of the engine) from before the engine starts, then `ok`, `failed …`, `failed_memory …` (section 4.3), `over_limit …` or `interrupted …`. On SIGINT, SIGTERM, SIGHUP and SIGQUIT the driver stops the engine with it and records the search as interrupted; a signal that comes while the engine starts takes effect once the engine's process group is known. A search is refused while its folder's status says `running` and that driver or engine is still alive (e.g. an engine left behind by a driver killed with SIGKILL).
  - **`count`** counts only a search whose status is `ok` and, where the folder has an `inputs` file (every search since 2026-11-13), whose inputs are the run table's. It skips any other search, and one whose result it cannot read (missing, cut, an accession not in the database), logs it as `# NOT COUNTED` with the reason and counts all others; the stage then ends with an error that lists them. It first removes an earlier count of the search and writes `count.tsv` only when `EntrapmentEvaluator` ends without error, so no old count survives a failure. Before, `count` stopped at the first missing result and took a folder without status as finished. The pilot folders of 2026-11-12 have no status file and are not counted until `ok` is written into them after a check, which must include their run ids, as they have no `inputs` file (the recounts of section 4.2 did so in copies).
  - **`collect`** writes `coverage.tsv`: every search the run table asks for (each engine, run or sample, and database) and every further search folder, with its status (`not_run` without a folder, `not_run_by_design` for Sage on the electron-based runs, `unknown` for a folder without a status file) and whether it was counted, for the comparisons over shared runs (section 4.2). Why a search was not counted is in `commands.log`. If no count exists, `collect` removes an old `counts.tsv`.
- **Selection and sharing:** `--only` takes run ids or sample names; a part of an immunopeptidomics sample is refused, as a sample is searched and counted as a whole. Several drivers may share a work directory only if they never run the same search folder, and never two MSFragger searches against the same database at once (its digest cache lies next to the database, section 4.3).
- **Stages of ProSE:** every ProSE search also writes its end-of-search report (`-summary_out`), which holds the times of its stages.
- **Engine logs:** since 2026-11-12 the output of every engine step is kept next to its results (`<stage>.log`, timed runs `<stage>.r<repetition>.log`), for example the tolerances the calibrations chose.
- **Further arms:** `search --extra '<settings>' --tag <name>` runs a further arm into its own result folder (`per_file_<name>`; `--extra` without `--tag` is refused). The settings are ProSE options (for example the `low_memory` arm of section 4.1, or ProSE + PeptDeep per instrument, section 4.4), Sage configuration keys as `key=JSON` (`deisotope=false`; the driver refuses a key that Sage 0.14.7 does not know, as Sage would ignore it) or MSFragger parameters as `key=value` (`calibrate_mass=0`; only keys of its parameter file). The keys are checked before any search folder is touched, also in a dry run (MSFragger's against the parameter file of its `--config closed`; a dry run without the MSFragger jar warns that they were not checked). Sage's `write_pin` and `annotate_matches` take `true` or `false` and become its flags `--write-pin` and `--annotate-matches` (since 2026-11-13): Sage 0.14.7 overwrites these two keys of its configuration with its command-line flags, so before, `--extra write_pin=true` was logged as applied but wrote no `.pin` (no count was affected). The Sage and MSFragger settings are logged in `commands.log`. Sage keeps the fragment tolerance of the parameter file in every arm (section 4.4; `--prose-fragment-tol` was removed on 2026-11-13).
- **Not scripted:** the external Percolator 3.09 control (section 4.2), MSFragger + MSBooster (section 4.4), and the summaries over the tables the driver writes (`counts.tsv`, `coverage.tsv`, `timings.tsv`): the comparisons over shared runs with the geometric mean of the per-run ratios (section 4.2), the mean ± SD of the FDP over the realisations (section 4.2), the medians of the repetitions, speed-up, efficiency and stage times (section 4.3), and the table of the settings each calibration chose per search, from the engine logs (section 4.4).
- **Smoke test:** [`../benchmarks/smoke/`](../benchmarks/smoke/) has its own run table: three small BSA runs from the OpenMS examples (LTQ Orbitrap XL, ion-trap CID, 10–14 MB each; fetched from OpenMS commit `4e7455e4`, the last that changed them, and checked against `smoke/vendor.sha256`) against 680 proteins of `hye` (the contaminants and 300 yeast proteins), with a plain and an entrapment database. It exercises every stage in minutes. The sample is one protein, so its identification numbers mean nothing.
- **Checked against the sources (2026-11-10):** the options of ProSE and `EntrapmentEvaluator` (OpenMS develop), the configuration of Sage 0.14.7, and the MSFragger parameter names and non-specific enzyme (FragPipe workflows).
- **Tested (2026-11-10)** on the smoke test with Sage 0.14.7, on a 20-core Linux machine:
  - **Run:** `databases`, `fetch`, `search` (timed and untimed, plain and entrapment databases), `batch`, `scaling` with pinning, `count` up to the idXML file, and `collect`. Sage accepted the configurations made from the parameter files of then, the unspecific ones included.
  - **Time limit:** a run over the limit is stopped together with its child processes and recorded.
  - **Found and fixed by the test:** Sage aborts on a single core (section 4.3), and a run over the limit in the discarded repetition left no record.
- **Tested (2026-11-11)** with the frozen OpenMS build (a native copy of the Docker image), Sage 0.14.7, MSFragger 4.4.1 and Percolator 3.09 (WSL2, Ubuntu 24.04, 20 cores; prose-paper `5c5dd5f`):
